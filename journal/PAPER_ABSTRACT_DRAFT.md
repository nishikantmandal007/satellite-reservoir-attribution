## Abstract

Reservoir operations represent a dominant but poorly quantified source of terrestrial water storage (TWS) variability in heavily managed river basins. Basin-scale satellite analyses — including GRACE-FO TWS decompositions — commonly attribute this variability to climate-forced groundwater and soil moisture dynamics, potentially mischaracterising the true driver. This study develops a multi-method framework to isolate and characterise anthropogenic storage signals in the Damodar Valley Corporation (DVC) reservoir cascade, Jharkhand–West Bengal, India. Monthly surface water area was reconstructed for Maithon Dam and Durgapur Barrage over 2017–2024 from Sentinel-1 C-band SAR using adaptive Otsu thresholding and JRC permanent water masking, and converted to storage anomalies via DEM-based area–volume hypsometry (RMSE vs. CWC validation: 0.07 m, n = 8). An XGBoost gradient boosting model, trained on eight non-collinear climate drivers with expanding-window cross-validation, predicted the climate-forced storage component (NSE = 0.208 on physically resolved rows; KGE = 0.226). The residual — the anthropogenic storage anomaly ΔS_anthro — ranged from −0.19 to +0.23 BCM (95% CI width ±0.05 BCM, bootstrap n = 50), with a mean of −0.03 BCM indicative of net management-driven drawdown. SHAP attribution identified reservoir operational inertia (lagged storage, mean |SHAP| = 0.064 BCM) and evaporative demand (PET, 0.043 BCM) as the dominant predictors; climate alone (no lag features) yielded NSE ≈ 0.02, confirming that managed operations, not climate, drive sub-annual storage variability. Convergent cross-mapping with 999 phase-randomised surrogates confirmed causal independence of ΔS_anthro from ΔS_climate (ρ = 0.04 vs surr_95th = 0.37) and detected significant hydraulic connectivity from Maithon to Durgapur (ρ = 0.29 > surr_95th = 0.14, converging). A Hidden Markov Model identified two operational regimes: DRAWDOWN (mean = −0.17 BCM, 25% of months) and FILL (+0.03 BCM, 75%). Dynamic Time Warping clustering of annual storage cycles yielded a four-archetype operational fingerprint taxonomy (silhouette = 0.49) using satellite data alone, without in-situ sensors or discharge records. Partial regression against GRACE-FO RL06.3 TWS anomalies yielded a significant coefficient for ΔS_anthro (β₂ = +3.84 cm/BCM, HAC-corrected p < 0.001, 95% CI [+2.1, +5.6]), consistent with the expected theoretical sensitivity of 4.28 cm/BCM for the Damodar basin area. These results demonstrate that DVC operations produce causally attributable storage signals detectable by multiple independent satellite methods, and that basin-scale GRACE assessments are likely to misattribute a substantial fraction of Damodar TWS variability to climate forcing. The operational fingerprint approach, applied using freely available Sentinel-1 and GRACE-FO data, offers a scalable tool for auditing reservoir-driven GRACE contamination across South Asian managed basins.

**Keywords:** reservoir operations; anthropogenic attribution; Damodar Valley Corporation; Sentinel-1 SAR; GRACE-FO; convergent cross-mapping; XGBoost; SHAP; operational fingerprint taxonomy; India water management
